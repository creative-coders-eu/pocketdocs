Privacy Policy

How Pocketdocs and Creative Coders handle personal data. The short answer: the Pocketdocs app doesn’t collect any, and we never see your documents.

In short

  • The Pocketdocs app doesn’t collect personal data, and we can’t see your documents. There’s nothing to sign up for, and we run no servers for the app.
  • Your documents are read and stored on your iPhone, encrypted, and locked with Face ID, Touch ID or your passcode.
  • If you turn on iCloud sync, an end-to-end encrypted copy is kept in your own iCloud, so your devices, and the people you choose to share with, can open it. We can’t read it, and neither can Apple.
  • Nothing leaves your iPhone unless you choose: iCloud sync and sharing, Copy to Mac or iPad, a backup, a copy you share, or a detail you fill in with AutoFill.
  • No analytics, ads, tracking or third-party SDKs in the app. No cookies, analytics or trackers on this website. The app’s App Store privacy label is “Data Not Collected”.
  • Apple processes all payments. We never see your payment details.
  • If you email us, we use your message only to help you. Please don’t send document details or pictures of documents.
  • You have rights under the GDPR, including the right to complain to the Polish data protection authority (UODO).

2. What this policy covers

This policy covers:

  • the Pocketdocs: ID & Doc Wallet app for iPhone, with its widgets and its AutoFill extension (“Pocketdocs”);
  • this website, creative-coders-eu.github.io/pocketdocs; and
  • emails you send us, and beta testing through TestFlight.

Apple’s own services, such as the App Store, your Apple Account, iCloud, Apple Intelligence and TestFlight, are covered by Apple’s Privacy Policy.

3. The app doesn’t collect personal data

  • Pocketdocs has no sign-up or sign-in of its own, and we run no servers for it. It doesn’t send your documents, how you use the app, or any identifier to us.
  • The app connects only to Apple: to the App Store, to check and make purchases, and, if you turn on iCloud sync, to your own iCloud. Everything else it does, including reading documents, the trip check and renewal checklists, happens on your iPhone.
  • It contains no third-party code: no analytics, advertising, attribution or crash-reporting SDKs.
  • It doesn’t track you across apps or websites, and it never asks for permission to do so.
  • That’s why Pocketdocs’ App Store privacy label is “Data Not Collected”.
  • We can’t see or access your documents unless you send them to us, and we can’t recover them for you. Keep your original documents safe (see Backups and new devices).

Because your documents are processed only on your devices and in your own iCloud, at your request and under your control, we don’t process them as a controller or as a processor.

4. What Pocketdocs stores on your iPhone

Pocketdocs keeps what you add, and what it needs to work, on your iPhone:

  • images of the document pages you scan or import from Photos or Files;
  • the details read from them or entered by you, such as names, document numbers, dates, issuing authorities and countries, and any notes you add;
  • for a document whose chip you read, the details the chip gave and the result of its signature check (see Reading a document’s chip);
  • for payment cards, the card details you keep. Pocketdocs never reads a card’s security code from a photo. If you type it in, it’s shown only after Face ID, Touch ID or your passcode, and it’s never offered in AutoFill or search;
  • the people whose documents you keep, and documents other people share with you;
  • your reminder choices and other settings.

This data is kept in Pocketdocs’ private storage on your iPhone and protected in layers:

  • iOS Data Protection at its strongest level (“complete”), so the files can’t be read while your iPhone is locked;
  • page images are also encrypted with AES-GCM, using a key kept in your iPhone’s Keychain;
  • the app can be locked with Face ID, Touch ID or your device passcode.

It stays until you delete it. You can edit or delete a document at any time, erase everything Pocketdocs stores with Delete all data in Pocketdocs’ settings, or delete the app. Copies outside Pocketdocs aren’t affected: the original pictures in Photos stay there, details you pasted into other apps stay in those apps, backup files you exported stay where you saved them, and older iPhone backups may still contain Pocketdocs data if you turned on Include in iPhone backup, until they’re replaced. If iCloud sync is on, deleting a document also deletes it from iCloud and from your other devices (see iCloud sync and family sharing).

5. Camera, Photos and Files

Pocketdocs uses the camera only while you scan a document, and iOS asks for your permission first. When you import from Photos or Files, Pocketdocs gets only the pictures or PDFs you choose. Images are processed on your iPhone. You can change camera access at any time in the iPhone Settings app under Apps, then Pocketdocs.

6. How documents are read

Pocketdocs reads documents on your iPhone with Apple’s Vision text recognition, including the machine-readable code lines on passports and ID cards and the barcodes on some cards. On iPhones that support Apple Intelligence, Pocketdocs also uses Apple’s on-device language model to help sort the details it finds. Pocketdocs doesn’t use any cloud or third-party AI service, and nothing from your documents is sent anywhere to read them.

Text recognition can make mistakes. Always compare the details with the original.

7. Reading a document’s chip

With Pocketdocs Pro, you can read the chip in a passport or ID card by holding your iPhone to it (NFC). To open the chip, Pocketdocs uses details from the document itself: the number, date of birth and expiry date, or the card access number (CAN) printed on an ID card.

  • Pocketdocs reads the chip’s personal details, the holder’s photo, and the chip’s signed security data, which it uses to check that the details are signed and unchanged.
  • The photo is shown with the result and isn’t saved. Pocketdocs saves the document’s key details from the chip, when it was read and the result of the check, on your iPhone, like any other detail.
  • Nothing read from the chip is sent anywhere. The check doesn’t prove that a document is genuine, and Pocketdocs doesn’t contact any authority.

8. Copying details

When you tap a detail, Pocketdocs copies it to this iPhone’s clipboard only and clears it after 60 seconds. If you touch and hold a detail and choose Copy to Mac or iPad, Pocketdocs uses Apple’s Universal Clipboard instead, so you can paste it on your own nearby Apple devices signed in to the same Apple Account; it also clears after 60 seconds. While a detail is on the clipboard, any app you paste into can read it, and once you paste it, that app handles it under its own terms.

9. AutoFill in Safari and apps

With Pocketdocs Pro, you can turn on Pocketdocs in the iPhone Settings app under General, then AutoFill & Passwords. You can then fill in a text field with a detail from your documents: touch and hold the field and choose AutoFill, then Passwords, then Pocketdocs.

  • So that AutoFill works while the app is closed, Pocketdocs keeps an encrypted list of your document details in a container that only Pocketdocs and its extensions can open. Its key is kept in a Keychain group that only the app and the AutoFill extension can use, on this iPhone only, and it can’t be opened while your iPhone is locked.
  • If the app lock is on, AutoFill asks for Face ID, Touch ID or your passcode first.
  • Only the detail you pick is inserted, into the field you chose. Pocketdocs stores no passwords and keeps no record of where you fill in details.
  • The list is deleted when you turn AutoFill off, when Pro ends, and with Delete all data.

10. iCloud sync and family sharing

iCloud sync is off until you turn it on in Pocketdocs (a Pro feature). When it’s on:

  • Pocketdocs keeps a copy of your documents in your own iCloud, in Pocketdocs’ private space in your iCloud account (Apple CloudKit), so your other devices signed in to the same Apple Account can open them. It uses your iCloud storage.
  • Every detail is stored with CloudKit’s end-to-end encryption, whose keys are in your iCloud Keychain. Page images are encrypted by Pocketdocs on your iPhone before they’re uploaded, with a key that is itself stored end-to-end encrypted. Apple stores this data but can’t read it; it can see technical information such as how many records there are, their size and when they changed. We receive nothing.
  • While sync is on, iCloud may wake Pocketdocs in the background with a silent notification when something changed on another device. These notifications contain no document details.
  • You can share one person’s documents with another Apple Account, for example your partner’s, and choose whether they can only view or also edit. Apple delivers the invitation, and the person you invite sees those documents and the name of the person they belong to. Stop sharing at any time, and the shared copies are removed from their devices. If someone shares documents with you, you can leave the share in the same way.
  • To stop, turn off Sync with iCloud. You can keep the iCloud copy or choose Turn off and remove iCloud copy, which deletes it from iCloud. Your documents stay on your iPhone either way.

iCloud is provided by Apple under its own terms and Privacy Policy. Apple isn’t our processor: you use iCloud under your agreement with Apple.

11. Widgets

With Pocketdocs Pro, you can add Pocketdocs widgets to your Home Screen and Lock Screen. They show your next expiry dates: each document’s type and title, whose it is, and when it expires. They never show document numbers. To keep the widgets up to date, Pocketdocs saves those few details in a small file that only Pocketdocs and its extensions can open. Widgets mark this content as private, so iOS can hide it on the Lock Screen while your iPhone is locked. You can remove a widget at any time.

12. Expiry reminders

Expiry reminders are local notifications scheduled on your iPhone. We don’t send them, and they don’t use a server. Their text names only the document type, such as “Passport”, never a name or a document number. Reminders are optional: you can turn them on or off at any time in the iPhone Settings app under Apps, then Pocketdocs.

13. Copies and pages you share

With Pocketdocs Pro, Make a copy creates a PDF of the pages you choose on your iPhone, with a watermark and the numbers you choose covered. You can also share a single page as a picture. Either leaves your iPhone only when you send it somewhere with the share sheet, and the app or person you send it to handles it under their own terms.

14. Backups and new devices

  • iPhone backup. By default, Pocketdocs data is left out of your iPhone backups, both iCloud Backup and backups on your computer. If you turn on Include in iPhone backup in Pocketdocs’ settings, it’s included in your own backup, which is controlled by you and Apple. We never receive it.
  • Backup files. You can export an encrypted backup file and save or send it wherever you like, for example to Files. It’s encrypted with AES-256-GCM using a password you choose, and nobody, including us, can open it without that password. We can’t recover a lost password.
  • New devices. To move to a new iPhone, use iCloud sync, a backup file or your iPhone backup with Include in iPhone backup on. Otherwise your documents stay on the old iPhone.

15. Purchases

Apple sells Pocketdocs Pro. Apple handles the personal data involved in purchases, payments, refunds and subscription management as an independent controller, under Apple’s Privacy Policy and the Apple Media Services Terms and Conditions. In the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland, that’s Apple Distribution International Ltd. in Ireland. We never receive your name, email address, Apple Account details or payment details. Pocketdocs checks what you’ve bought on your iPhone using Apple’s StoreKit.

Apple gives us sales and subscription reports. They can include your country and a subscriber number that Apple creates just for us, but not your name or contact details, so we can’t tell who you are. We use these reports only for accounting and tax, which the law requires (GDPR Art. 6(1)(c)), and to understand how Pocketdocs Pro is doing, which is our legitimate interest (Art. 6(1)(f)). We keep them as long as accounting and tax law requires.

16. Crash reports from Apple

If you’ve chosen to share analytics with app developers (in the iPhone Settings app: Privacy & Security → Analytics & Improvements → Share With App Developers), Apple may give us crash reports and aggregated statistics, such as how often the app is used or crashes. They don’t contain your documents, and they don’t identify you to us.

We use them to find and fix problems and, in aggregate, to understand how Pocketdocs is used. That’s based on our legitimate interest in keeping Pocketdocs reliable and improving it (GDPR Art. 6(1)(f)). We delete crash reports we download within 12 months. You can stop sharing at any time in the same setting.

17. Beta testing with TestFlight

If you test Pocketdocs through Apple’s TestFlight, Apple runs the beta under its own privacy policy. If we invite you by email, we enter your name and email address in App Store Connect so that Apple can send you the invitation. If you join through a public link, Apple doesn’t show us your name or email address. TestFlight automatically collects crash reports and usage information, such as your device model, iOS version, installs and sessions, and Apple makes them available to us together with any feedback, screenshots and comments you send. Apple keeps feedback for a year, and may keep crash reports and usage information until the problems are fixed.

We use this only to run the beta and fix problems (GDPR Art. 6(1)(b) and (f)). Apart from the email invitation described above, we don’t share it, and we delete our copies within 12 months after the beta ends. Before you send a screenshot, check that it doesn’t show a real document or its details.

18. When you email us

  • What: your email address, your name if you include it, your message and any attachments.
  • Why: to answer you and to handle your request or complaint.
  • Legal basis: taking steps under our agreement with you (GDPR Art. 6(1)(b)), our legitimate interest in answering people who contact us (Art. 6(1)(f)), and our legal duty to handle complaints (Art. 6(1)(c)).
  • Please don’t send document details. We can help without your document numbers or pictures of your documents, so please don’t attach them or screenshots that show them. If you send them anyway, we’ll help without using them and delete them once we’ve answered.
  • Who handles it: our email provider, Zoho Corporation B.V. (the Netherlands), which hosts our mailbox in its EU data centres and acts as our processor.
  • How long: we delete support emails 24 months after your last message, unless we need them longer to establish, exercise or defend a legal claim.

19. This website

  • This website uses no cookies, analytics, tracking pixels, external fonts or third-party scripts. It’s plain HTML and CSS.
  • It’s hosted on GitHub Pages by GitHub, Inc. in the United States. When you visit, your browser sends GitHub your IP address and standard request details, and GitHub logs visitors’ IP addresses for security purposes. We don’t receive these logs. The legal basis is our legitimate interest in running a secure website (GDPR Art. 6(1)(f)). See GitHub’s Privacy Statement.
  • Our pages ask your browser not to tell other sites that you came from here when you follow a link.
  • Other websites we link to, such as Apple’s, are covered by their own policies.

20. Who receives personal data

We don’t sell or rent personal data, and we don’t use it for advertising. Recipients are limited to:

  • Zoho, our email provider (support emails), acting as our processor;
  • GitHub, our website host (technical data from your visit);
  • Apple Inc., which runs TestFlight (the name and email address of beta testers we invite by email; see Beta testing with TestFlight);
  • public authorities, if the law requires us to disclose information.

Apple isn’t our processor. It acts as an independent controller, under its own privacy policy, for the App Store, purchases, iCloud, TestFlight, crash reports, backups and Apple Intelligence. The people you choose to share documents with receive those documents from you, through iCloud, not from us.

If we ever add a service provider that receives personal data, it will have to protect it at least as well as described here, and we’ll update this policy first.

International transfers

Zoho stores our mailbox in the EU. According to Zoho, its staff outside the European Economic Area can access data only in rare technical-support cases, under the European Commission’s standard contractual clauses. GitHub is based in the United States; it complies with the EU–US Data Privacy Framework and also relies on standard contractual clauses. Apple Inc. is also based in the United States. When we invite a beta tester by email, we give Apple the tester’s name and email address because that’s necessary to provide the beta the tester asked to join (GDPR Art. 49(1)(b)). You can ask us for details of these safeguards at any time.

22. Your rights

For personal data we hold about you, such as your emails, the GDPR gives you the right to:

  • access it and get a copy;
  • have it corrected;
  • have it deleted;
  • restrict how we use it;
  • receive it in a portable format;
  • object to processing based on our legitimate interests;
  • withdraw your consent at any time, without affecting what we did before.

We don’t make decisions about you based solely on automated processing.

To use these rights, email admin@ccoders.eu. We’ll answer within one month, or tell you within that month why we need up to two more. We may ask you to confirm it’s you, but we’ll never ask for a copy of an identity document.

Your documents in Pocketdocs never reach us, so you manage them yourself: you can view, change, export and delete them in the app at any time, and remove your iCloud copy there too.

You don’t have to give us any personal data, and Pocketdocs works without it. If you email us, we need your email address to reply; without it, we can’t answer.

Complaints

You can lodge a complaint with the President of the Personal Data Protection Office (Prezes Urzędu Ochrony Danych Osobowych, UODO), ul. Stanisława Moniuszki 1A, 00-014 Warszawa, Poland, uodo.gov.pl. You can also complain to the data protection authority in the EU country where you live or work, or, in the UK, to the Information Commissioner’s Office (ico.org.uk). We’d welcome the chance to put things right first, so please email us too.

23. Information for US residents

  • We don’t sell personal information, share it for advertising or use it for profiling.
  • We don’t track you, so a Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track signal from your browser has nothing left to switch off.
  • Apart from the security logs GitHub keeps when you visit this website (see This website) and the statistics Apple collects if you choose to share analytics with app developers (see Crash reports from Apple), no third party collects personal information about your online activities over time and across websites or apps through Pocketdocs or this website.
  • We don’t use personal data to train AI models.
  • You can use the rights in Your rights wherever you live. If we decline a request, you can appeal by replying to our answer with the word “Appeal”. We’ll respond in writing within 45 days, and if we deny your appeal, you can complain to your state attorney general.

24. Children

Pocketdocs isn’t directed to children, and we don’t knowingly collect personal data from children. With Pocketdocs Pro, you can keep your children’s documents; like everything else in Pocketdocs, they stay on your devices and in your own iCloud, and never reach us. If you think a child has sent us personal data, email us and we’ll delete it.

25. Security

  • We run no servers for Pocketdocs, so there’s no central database of identity documents on our side to break into.
  • Your documents are protected by iOS Data Protection, page images are encrypted again with a key kept in your iPhone’s Keychain, and the app can be locked with Face ID, Touch ID or your passcode. Use a passcode on your iPhone.
  • With iCloud sync on, every detail is end-to-end encrypted and page images are encrypted before they leave your iPhone.
  • Backup files are encrypted with your password, and the AutoFill list with a key that never leaves this iPhone.
  • Details you copy clear from the clipboard after 60 seconds.
  • Only the people who answer support emails can read them, and our email provider only when it needs to for technical support.
  • This website is served over HTTPS and loads no third-party code.
